Gofer is a social wishlist that learns your tastes over time. The more it learns about your taste, the better your recommendations, and the better the gifts you get from friends.

I started in September 2025. Nick had already started the app. My plan was to design in Figma and hand the designs off. I underestimated what a solid native app takes, so I taught myself to build it with Claude.

The problem

People find products everywhere. Instagram, stores, a friend's text, screenshots. Saving them is a mess. And most recs come from an algorithm or a paid influencer.

So the idea was one place to save anything you want. Your friends can see it, and it learns what you like.

How the idea changed

It didn't start as a wishlist. We assumed friends would want to share the things they loved with other friends. If you loved your standing desk, you'd share it, and maybe get rewarded for it. But we couldn't figure out a good way to get friends to do that.

So it slowly became more of a social shopping cart and wishlist. It made it much easier to share things you haven't bought yet but are looking to buy. Better, but a plain list doesn't learn anything about you.

Now it learns your taste first, through the Taste Tab, and the wishlist sits on top.

What I built

The Taste Tab

A short quiz that builds your taste profile. I designed the flow, built the quiz screens in the app, and picked the 490 products the quiz shows across seven categories. The server side that turns your answers into recommendations was mostly built by others. I made smaller changes there.

This is the part that gave me a lot more respect for engineers. Each quiz had products on its cover, and once you took it, the cover changed to show what you picked. Simple as a concept, hard to build.

DigUI, a design system in SwiftUI

Cards, buttons, badges and loading states. I built it from scratch so screens share the same pieces.

In 2019 I designed components and engineers built them. On Gofer I built them myself.

Onboarding and analytics

We cut onboarding from about 11 steps to about 8, and I rebuilt it in code. I also built the app side of passwordless email sign-in. Nick had already set up PostHog. I added funnels so we could see where people dropped off, and set session replay to hide anything private.

The marketing site

The first version was generated with an AI site builder before I joined. I reworked it: a waitlist, analytics, cookie consent, and an A/B test setup for the landing page. The illustrations were made in Midjourney to a style guide I wrote.

How I worked

My process changed. I used to start in Mobbin for inspiration, design in Figma, and find out late whether it actually worked. On Gofer, Claude pulled references from Mobbin and gave me several concepts. I picked one, refined it, and it got built straight in code. I rarely opened Figma.

Most of my code was written with Claude. I told it what to build and checked it in the app myself.

Where it ended up

Gofer soft-launched on the App Store on August 5, 2026. The first users were mostly friends, family and testers.

What I took from it

I underestimated the work. I thought I'd design it and hand it off. Instead I had to learn how to build it. But I learned how to learn.

It also changed what I think a designer's job is. If I'm building and shipping it, I need to know where the data comes from and what iOS can do. It's really a design engineer role.
